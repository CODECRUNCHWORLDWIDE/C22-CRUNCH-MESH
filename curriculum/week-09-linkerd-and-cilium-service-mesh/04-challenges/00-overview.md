# Week 9 — Challenges

The exercises produce the numbers. **The challenge makes you defend a decision.** It's a mock staff design review: you've recommended a mesh, and a panel of three skeptical senior engineers is going to push on your reasoning the way a real review does. The skill isn't installing a mesh — it's holding a position under cross-examination, conceding what's genuinely uncertain, and reversing only when the argument actually warrants it.

## Index

1. **[Challenge 1 — Defend the mesh choice](./challenge-01-defend-the-mesh-choice.md)** — you bring an ADR recommending one of the three meshes (or no mesh) for a hypothetical org, and respond in writing to three pointed objections: a cost objection, a feature objection, and a "why not just use the popular one" objection. You must answer each with evidence (your benchmark, the lecture trade-offs), concede where the objection has merit, and either hold your recommendation or change it — and justify whichever you do. (~90 min)

Challenges are optional for passing the week, but this one is the single best rehearsal for the midterm architecture-review essay (end of Week 12) and the mock staff system-design interview (Week 23). A mesh recommendation that can't survive three obvious objections isn't a recommendation — it's a preference. The engineer who can defend a position with numbers, concede the real trade-offs, and name exactly what would change their mind is the one who gets to make the call. This challenge is that conversation, rehearsed before it counts.
