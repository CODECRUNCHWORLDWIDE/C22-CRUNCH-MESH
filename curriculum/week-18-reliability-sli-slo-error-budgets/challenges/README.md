# Week 18 — Challenges

The exercises drill the mechanics. **The challenge makes you the on-call engineer in the middle of a cascade.** Payment had a 30-second blip this morning — nothing, a routine hiccup it recovered from on its own. And yet for the next twenty minutes cart, inventory, *and* the BFF were all down, the dashboards were a wall of red, and payment — which had recovered — was being hammered back into the ground every time it tried to come up. A 30-second dependency blip became a 20-minute system-wide outage, and the dependency that caused it wasn't even broken anymore. You have to find why a tiny transient turned into a self-sustaining catastrophe, and fix the *amplification* — because the blip was never the problem.

## Index

1. **[Challenge 1 — The retry storm that took down payment](challenge-01-the-retry-storm-that-took-down-payment.md)** — a 30-second payment blip cascades into a 20-minute outage because un-jittered, unbudgeted retries plus a missing circuit breaker turned every caller into a synchronized thundering herd that kept knocking the recovering dependency back down. Using the metrics and traces from Week 17, you must (a) prove the outage outlasted the original fault — the amplification is the bug, not the blip — (b) name the exact mechanism (synchronized retries + no breaker = retry storm), and (c) fix it with jitter, a retry budget, and a circuit breaker, then demonstrate the same blip is now a non-event. (~90 min)

Challenges are optional for passing the week, but this one is the single most realistic reliability on-call scenario there is. Cascading failure from retry amplification is *the* way distributed systems die — a small fault becomes a large outage not because the fault was big but because the system *amplified* it. The engineer who can look at a 20-minute outage caused by a 30-second blip and say "this is a retry storm — we have no jitter, no retry budget, and no circuit breaker; the blip recovered but our retries kept re-killing it" is the one who turns the next blip into a non-event. The team that *can't* keeps having 30-second problems cause 30-minute outages, and never understands why "it recovered on its own" wasn't the end of the story.
